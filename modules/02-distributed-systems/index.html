Distributed Systems for AI Infrastructure

Learn to reason about partial failure, coordination, consistency, messaging, retries, idempotency, backpressure, and recovery in systems that span agents, model providers, queues, databases, and regions.

Core mental model

Networks turn ordinary failures into ambiguous failures

In a single process, success and failure are usually visible. Across a network, a timeout cannot tell you whether work failed, succeeded, or is still running. Distributed design begins by accepting uncertainty.

Partial failure

One dependency can fail while the rest of the system remains healthy. Isolate failures and define degraded modes.

Time and ordering

Clock skew, delayed messages, and retries make global ordering expensive. Use logical versions and explicit causality where needed.

Delivery semantics

At-most-once, at-least-once, and effectively-once behaviours are application contracts, not marketing labels.

Consistency

Choose consistency per invariant. Strong consistency is valuable for ownership and money; eventual consistency is often enough for search and telemetry.

Backpressure

When consumers slow down, producers must block, shed load, buffer within limits, or degrade. Unbounded queues only postpone failure.

Recovery

Recovery requires durable state, replay boundaries, idempotent handlers, checkpoints, and operator-visible progress.

Decision framework

Start from invariants, not technology

  1. Define the invariantWhat must never be wrong, duplicated, lost, or observed out of order?
  2. Define failure boundariesWhich components, regions, tenants, and workflows may fail independently?
  3. Choose the state ownerIdentify the authoritative service and avoid ambiguous shared ownership.
  4. Choose communicationUse synchronous calls for immediate answers and asynchronous messaging for decoupling and recovery.
  5. Design replayAssume duplicates, delayed messages, retries, and restarts from the beginning.

Interview preparation

Principal-level questions

How do you make retries safe?

Discuss idempotency keys, deduplication windows, state transitions, side-effect isolation, retry budgets, and observability.

Kafka or RabbitMQ?

Compare replay, ordering, routing, throughput, consumer state, operational maturity, and the actual workload.

How do you prevent queue collapse?

Bound producer rates, scale consumers, prioritize traffic, expire stale work, isolate tenants, and shed load before saturation.

Strong or eventual consistency?

Anchor the choice to business invariants, user-visible consequences, latency, availability, and reconciliation strategy.

How do you coordinate agents?

Separate durable workflow state, transient messages, ownership leases, checkpoints, compensation, and human escalation.

How do you debug distributed latency?

Use traces, queue age, dependency timing, retries, saturation, clock-aware correlation, and tail-latency decomposition.
